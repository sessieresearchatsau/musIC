Approaches to concatenating a long set
          what the labels mean · results on the corpus

        

          
One set has many indexed concatenations that expand back to it exactly.
            Which one a search returns comes down to three choices, and each approach is
            named by them: how a block is chosen · how levels are built ·
            what € may do.

          

            
How a block is chosen

            
GGreedy-shortest. The shortest repeating
              pattern is fixed first and never revisited (ReduceSetList). A small block can
              stop a bigger one from being found.

            
SBest-saving. Every candidate is scored and
              the one that saves the most wins.

            
BBar-anchored. No piece crosses a bar line,
              and each bar is one part (a € of one copy, which is the bar itself:
              €(i,0,0)[x] = x), so the levels above group whole
              bars, then phrases.

            
VVariant-merging. Repeats that differ by a
              piece still line up; where the piece is absent its count is 0.

            
How levels are built

            
FFlat, then nested. The biggest block in the
              raw rows first, then the search goes inside its body.

            
UUp, level by level. Short pieces, then groups
              of pieces, then groups of those.

            
What € may do

            
E1Repeat and index, with polynomial formulas:
              €(i,0,1)[{69-2i, 32}].

            
E2A count that comes out 0 or 1 in some repeats,
              switching a piece off or writing it once:
              €(i,0,2-3j) is 3 rows at j = 0 and none at j = 1.

            
E3A € inside a number, as a term of a sum. It adds
              what it expands to, and nothing when it is empty:
              €(n,0,3)[67 + €(n-2)[10]] is 67, 67, 67, 77 —
              the first note of each phrase of Happy Birthday, without the cubic
              67 + 10n(n-1)(n-2)/6. Mathematica agrees:
              Plus[67, Sequence[]] is 67. Only + may join it.

            
E4A € inside a row: {1, €(3)[3]}
              is {1, 3, 3, 3}.

          

          
The gates of E2 and E3 come in three kinds, each a € whose count is a formula
            in the repeat's index j: a step €(j-2)[d] adds d
            once j passes 2; a one-off €(1-(j-2)^2)[d] adds d
            at j = 2 only; and an alternation (-1)^j. The
            approaches with E weigh a number that changes between repeats at a tenth of a
            part (S·U weighs it at 0.4), since otherwise a gate is never worth its numbers.
